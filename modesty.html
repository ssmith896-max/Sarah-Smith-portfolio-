Modesty

Sarah Smith

View original PDF ↗

Cedarville has a standard—or does it? When it comes to modesty, the topic can be difficult to define. There appears to be a general standard of modesty on campus, but where does that standard come from? For Laura Ball, the answer is not as simple as it may seem. Ball is a junior special education major at Cedarville University, captain of the Cedarville University speech and debate forensics team and an employee at Career Services. As a military kid, she grew up with a variety of cultural perspectives on modesty. However, she felt stressed when preparing to come to Cedarville because of the dress code in the handbook at the start of her freshman year. “From my experience when I first came to Cedarville, I left a lot more clothes at home that I bring and wear now, because just reading Cedarville's dress code and not actually being here to experience it.” Ball grew up in what she described as a looser environment, and Cedarville’s handbook seemed more conservative than her personal views. She said, “I think Cedarville is largely highly modest.” However, a survey of Cedarville female students suggested a different conclusion. Many of the young women said they did not feel forced into the dress code. Because they did not see the handbook’s expectations enforced outside of class, they did not view them as a problem. Cedarville’s culture seemed to hold a standard, but the handbook was not necessarily the enforcer of it. Ball discovered after coming to Cedarville that the dress expectations were often connected to professionalism. “I think Cedarville only enforces dress code largely in classes, and so you can normally tell outside of classes which students grew up outside of that conservative, more conservative part of the spectrum.” The Cedarville University Student Handbook supports this connection, stating that “our campus culture supports the professional development of students and allows faculty to communicate additional dress guidelines for classes that are consistent with the course objectives.” Ball continued, “Yeah, and so I think dressing is an easy way that professors and faculty have used in the past and continue to use to build us up as professionals, and I think sometimes we equate that with Cedarville's modesty.” Modesty culture, then, may extend beyond what the handbook formally enforces. Students come from a variety of backgrounds, and those differences can shape how they understand the university’s expectations. Rather than treating clothing choices only as a matter of modesty, students may also need to consider how their appearance communicates professionalism. While students may view Cedarville’s dress expectations as mandates about modesty, the handbook frames them partly as professional development. This clarification matters because students arrive at Cedarville with different backgrounds and definitions of modesty, which can shape how they interpret the same handbook policy. For Ball, what once felt like a restrictive dress code became a clearer picture of the values behind Cedarville’s culture. Laura Ball, Captain of Cedarville forensics team, October 1, 2026 Cedarville University. Graduate and Online Student Handbook 2025–26. 2025, https://publications.cedarville.edu/brochures/studentlife/graduatestudenthandbook/17/.
