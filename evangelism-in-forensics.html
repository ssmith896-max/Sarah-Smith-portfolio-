Evangelism in Forensics

Sarah Smith

View original PDF ↗

When an animal lives in a cage or pen, protection can limit its exposure to the world beyond its enclosure. A similar question can be asked about students at Cedarville University. Does living in a gospel-centered environment prepare them to engage with the outside world, or leave them disconnected and sheltered from it? For Cedarville’s forensics speech and debate team, tournaments provide opportunities to step outside that bubble. Through competition and conversations, team members encounter different beliefs while finding opportunities to share their own. Joshua Parker, a senior on the team, said he has seen the Lord work through his experiences at tournaments. “I've only seen one person be saved at a tournament, but every single response I've gotten has been positive,” Parker said. Referring to the times that he has personally brought up his faith and shared the gospel. During his three years on the team, Parker has sought to share the gospel through conversations with competitors. He had an opportunity to share his faith with a competitor after a speech round. His experience illustrates how interactions surrounding competition can become opportunities for conversations about Christianity. Joshua Parker had an evangelism moment at the last tournament the team went to in Illinois he said, “This guy's name was Diego was curious, and so we talked about like 15 other minutes as we were waiting for the impromptu round to keep going, and it came back to me somehow making a comment about how, man, I'm so glad our value isn't found in our speeches, and that's not where our worth comes from.” Parker contentious, “He's like, "What do you mean?" And I said, "Well, I think that how competitively well we do doesn't actually define our value overall, and that we can be secure even if we suck." And he's like, "Well, where does our value come from then?" And I said, "God," Laura Ball, a junior and the team’s captain, described two ways these opportunities develop. “So, in terms of the way I've seen the gospel have opportunities to be spread at tournaments. The two primary ways are engagement in strange conversations and just the relationships that we get to start at tournaments and how we can leverage those post-tournament and in future tournaments.” Ball continues, “Like I still have people from my high school forensics tournaments like to swipe up on my Instagram story and talk to me about things. We invited OU to come to chapel last year when we hosted states, and they almost came, which like that wouldn't happen without the relationships at tournaments.” For Ball, one conversation began with a discussion about her coursework. After she mentioned taking a biblical womanhood class, a group of students from Ohio University wanted to know more. “So I kind of just gave them an overview of my class, what we were talking about, and they ended up getting really invested in the class, and they started asking me follow-up questions specifically about like Adam and Eve's creation, and how that outlines like what a woman is, and the fact that the woman came from the man, and all those really fun questions you secretly hope no one asks you ever,” Ball said. Her experience shows how discussing everyday student life can lead to deeper questions about faith. Those questions may be challenging, but they also give team members opportunities to explain their beliefs and listen to others. Coach Mishne emphasized that the team’s goals include competing well and allowing God’s glory to be seen through their conduct. Competitive success and Christian witness are both part of the team’s purpose. Parker and Ball approach these conversations through their experiences, but both describe tournaments as places where faith can be shared. Their accounts challenge the idea that Cedarville’s protected environment prevents students from connecting with people outside it. For the forensics team, leaving campus to compete means entering conversations that extend beyond trophies, rankings and speeches. One tournament at a time, students are putting their faith into conversation with the wider world. Sources: Joshua Parker, senior, Cedarville University forensics team — interview material provided. Laura Ball, junior and captain, Cedarville University forensics team — interview material provided. Coach Mishne, Cedarville University forensics team — paraphrased comments provided.
